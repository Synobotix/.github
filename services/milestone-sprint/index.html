PROOF · ANONYMIZED

Four weeks to a demo that held

The situation. A UAS team had a perception model that ran fine on a workstation and missed its control loop budget on the aircraft, with a customer flight three weeks out.

What we did. Instrumented the whole path first, found most of the loss outside the model, then reworked the graph and quantized what tolerance allowed. A deterministic wrapper went in behind it with a defined fail safe.

Where it landed. Budget met on the board with accuracy inside their tolerance, published benchmarks their customer could check, and a HIL suite the team kept using after the flight.

QUESTIONS WE GET

What do you need from us to start?

The model and its evaluation set, the target board, access to hardware and data from day one, a technical contact who answers within 24 hours, and a reproducible build. Two units of the hardware on our bench makes week one twice as fast.

What if the bottleneck is not the model?

That is a common outcome and the audit usually catches it. We rescope onto the real bottleneck before you sign, or tell you it is outside what we do.

Will you touch accuracy?

Only inside a tolerance you set in the scope, and every step is measured against your own evaluation set so you can reject any of them.

Can the sprint be extended?

It closes at week four by design. If more work is warranted we scope a second sprint or move to a retainer, as a new decision with a new price.

Who does the work?

One or two senior engineers from our team. You meet them in the audit and they stay to the handover.

Can we publish the results?

Yes, the benchmarks and the method are yours. We only reference your program publicly if you clear it in writing.

Send the model and the budget it has to hit

Model, target board, the number you are missing and the date. Within 48 hours you get the bottleneck, a realistic target and a fixed price for the four weeks.

THE SAFETY LAYER

A stochastic policy inside a deterministic envelope

The learned policy proposes. A hand-written, fully testable layer decides what the actuators are allowed to do. That boundary is what turns “the AI drove it” into something you can put in a document and defend.

STAGE 01

Sensor intake

Timestamped, rate bounded, dropped frames explicitly accounted for.

STAGE 02

Policy inference

Running inside a fixed compute budget, with tail latency instrumented.

STAGE 03 · DETERMINISTIC

Safety wrapper

Envelope checks, rate limits, watchdog, defined fail safe state. No learning inside this box.

STAGE 04

Actuation and log

Every decision replayable on the HIL bench, months later.

SELECTED WORK · ANONYMIZED

From requirement to demonstrator for a UGV integrator

The situation. A ground robotics integrator had a manipulation requirement, no model, and a customer demonstration eleven weeks out. Nothing in the stack bounded what the arm could do if a policy went wrong.

What we did. Two of our engineers embedded with their team: architecture and data strategy first, then the policy itself, then a deterministic wrapper with explicit joint envelopes, rate limits and a defined fail-safe pose. Validated on a HIL bench they kept.

Where it landed. A demonstrator that ran the full customer script on board, a wrapper spec their safety reviewer could read, and two of their own engineers able to retrain and redeploy it without us.
